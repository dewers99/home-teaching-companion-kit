# Pre-K supplies & materials reference

**Version:** 1.0.0

*This appendix holds the recommended supplies and materials for ages 3–5 (Pre-K / preschool), organized so the companion can build a tailored checklist or shopping list for any budget. Consulted when the parent asks what to buy, gather, or set up — never volunteered in full unprompted. Every item carries three paths: buy it, a budget tip, and a household alternative made by recycling or repurposing. Nothing here is required — start small and add as the child shows interest.*

## How the companion uses this appendix

- Ask about **budget** before recommending: tight, moderate, or "buy the good stuff." Never assume.
- Build a **short starter list first** (the Minimal starter kit below), then offer the rest by category. A parent who buys everything at once usually regrets half of it.
- Always lead with the **household alternative** when the budget is tight — repurposing is a feature, not a compromise.
- Suggest sourcing in this order: things the family already owns → Buy Nothing / thrift / dollar store → buy new.

## Minimal starter kit

If the parent wants the smallest useful set, start here — everything else is optional:

- Crayons and washable markers
- Safety scissors and glue sticks
- Paper (plain printer paper plus whatever scrap comes in the mail)
- Playdough (homemade or store-bought) with a few tools
- Counting/sorting objects from the kitchen (dried beans, bottle caps, buttons)
- A basket of picture books (library counts)
- Balls and outdoor play basics the family likely owns already
- Bins or baskets to keep it all contained

## Sourcing on a budget

In order of cheapest first:
1. **The house itself** — run the household-alternative column before spending anything.
2. **Buy Nothing groups and neighborhood parent groups** — ask for craft supplies, puzzles, and outgrown toys; parents give these away constantly.
3. **Thrift stores** — puzzles, books, dress-up clothes, baskets; check the kids' section and the housewares aisle.
4. **Dollar stores** — crayons, markers, glue sticks, bins, stickers.
5. **Library** — books first, but also many libraries lend puzzles, STEM kits, and instruments.
6. **Buy new last** — and only the item the child will actually use this month.

## Safety notes

- **Under age 3:** no small parts — buttons, beads, and small caps are choking hazards. Stick to larger alternatives (big blocks, large lids, chunky crayons).
- Choose **non-toxic, washable** art materials; check age labels on packaging.
- Supervise scissors, and store them out of reach when not in use.
- Inspect recycled materials: no sharp edges, staples, residue, or allergens (peanut-butter jars, latex).
- Sensory bin fillers like dried beans are for supervised play only with mouth-exploring toddlers.

## Honest limits

This appendix reflects commonly recommended Pre-K materials and widely shared budget practices — not professional safety or medical advice. Age labels on commercial products exist for a reason; when in doubt, follow them. If the parent has concerns about a material's safety, the companion should say so plainly and suggest they check with the manufacturer or their pediatrician.
