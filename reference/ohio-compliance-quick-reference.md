# Ohio home-education quick reference

**Version:** 1.0.0
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

*This appendix holds the verified legal facts the companion consults when the user asks about Ohio home-education requirements. It is consulted on request — never volunteered in full unprompted. Facts verified 2026-09-27. This is public legal information, not legal advice.*

## Governing law

- Ohio Revised Code **3321.042**, effective **October 3, 2023** (enacted via House Bill 33, 135th General Assembly).
- Official text: codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-3321.042
- State authority: the Ohio Department of Education & Workforce.

## Key definitions

- **Home education** = parent-directed education of a child **ages 6–18**.
- Compulsory attendance ages in Ohio: **6–18**. A child under 6 has **no notification obligation**.
- A child receiving home education in the required subject areas is **exempt** from compulsory attendance (ORC 3321.04).

## Required subject areas (6)

1. English language arts
2. Mathematics
3. Science
4. History
5. Government
6. Social studies

## Notification procedure

- Send notice to the **superintendent of the district of residence**:
  - **Within 5 calendar days** of starting home education, moving into a new district, or withdrawing from a school; and
  - **Annually by August 30** thereafter.
- The notice contains **only**:
  - Parent's name and address
  - Child's name
  - Assurance that the child will receive education in the required subject areas above
- The exemption is **effective immediately upon receipt** of the notice.
- The superintendent must send **written acknowledgment within 14 calendar days**. Keep that letter — it is the year's official record of the exemption.

## Re-enrolling in public school

A child returning to public school after home education is placed in the appropriate grade level **without discrimination or prejudice**, per district policy.

## Eliminated by the October 2023 change

Do **not** present these as current Ohio law:

- The 900-hour annual instruction minimum
- Annual assessments, standardized testing, or portfolio reviews
- Teacher qualification requirements
- Curriculum outline submission to the district
- Detailed record-keeping mandates

## Outdated-guide warning

A large share of online guides — and some official-looking pages — still describe the **pre-October-2023** rules. Teach this heuristic: **if a guide mentions 900 hours, annual assessments, or teacher qualifications for Ohio, it is outdated.** Check the guide's date and verify against ORC 3321.042.

## Recommended records (not legally required)

Ohio does not mandate record-keeping, but keeping simple records is still wise:

- A copy of each year's notification
- The superintendent's written acknowledgment letter
- Per-child progress logs (see `templates/progress-log.template.md`)

These help with re-enrollment, College Credit Plus applications, and personal organization.

## Graduation & diplomas

Ohio law sets no graduation requirements for home education: no mandated courses, credits, tests, or state-issued diploma. The parent determines when the student has completed their education and issues the diploma. Keep the transcript and course descriptions — colleges, employers, and the military ask for those, not a state credential. See `reference/9-12-subjects.md` for transcript guidance.
