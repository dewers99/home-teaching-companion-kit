# Texas home-education quick reference

**Version:** 1.0.1
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

*This appendix holds the verified legal facts the companion consults when the user asks about Texas home-education requirements. It is consulted on request — never volunteered in full unprompted. Facts verified 2026-09-27. This is public legal information, not legal advice.*

## Governing law

- Compulsory attendance: **TEC §25.085**. Exemption: **TEC §25.086(a)(1)** — a child "attends a private or parochial school that includes in its course a study of good citizenship."
- ***Texas Education Agency v. Leeper*, 893 S.W.2d 432 (Tex. 1994)** (Texas Supreme Court): children taught at home fall within the §25.086(a)(1) private-school exemption. (TEA's own "Alternative Schooling" page dates this to 1995 — the opinion is 1994.)
- State authority: Texas Education Agency (TEA) — which states it "does not regulate, index, monitor, approve, register, or accredit" home-school programs.

## Key definitions

- Compulsory attendance: **age 6 (as of September 1) until the 19th birthday**. Also covers a child under 6 previously enrolled in first grade, and a child enrolled in public pre-K or kindergarten.
- A qualifying home school pursues, **in a bona fide (good faith, not sham) manner**, a curriculum of **books, workbooks, or other written materials (including electronic/computer screens)**, from any source, designed to meet basic education goals.

## Required subjects (the *Leeper* five)

Reading, spelling, grammar, mathematics, and a study of good citizenship — pursued bona fide with written materials. **"Writing" is not in the court's list**; guides that add it are wrong.

## Notification procedure

- **None.** No notification to TEA, the district, or anyone is required.
- **Withdrawing from public school:** do it **in writing** — sign the withdrawal forms or a signed and dated letter stating the student is being homeschooled and the start date (Leaver Reason Code 60), per the 2019 Commissioner's Home School Policy Letter. No in-person appearance or curriculum review is permitted. This prevents truancy filings.
- Districts may **request in writing** a letter of assurance from parents they believe are homeschooling — reactive and district-initiated, not an annual filing duty. No statute penalizes non-response; consequences are untested — check the official source rather than treating this as settled.

## Testing

None. The *Leeper* judgment's clause that TEA *may suggest* lawful methods including test-score inquiry is permissive dictum TEA has never exercised.

## Records

None mandated. Keep the withdrawal letter and any district correspondence.

## Teacher qualifications

None — no statute, TEA rule, or the *Leeper* decision imposes any credential, degree, or certification.

## Graduation & diplomas

Parent-determined. "The State of Texas does not award diplomas to homeschooled students." **TEC §51.9241**: Texas treats successful completion of a nontraditional secondary education (including home school) as **equivalent to public-high-school graduation**; Texas colleges must admit such applicants under the same general standards and may not require a GED or extra exams.

## Re-enrolling in public school

The district assesses mastery for grade placement and credit (same treatment as unaccredited-private-school transfers — credit by curriculum/work review or assessment; secondary credit-by-exam held to a 70% standard). Districts must let home-school students take the PSAT/NMSQT and AP exams offered to enrolled students.

## Outdated-guide warnings

- Dating *Leeper* to 1995 — it's 1994.
- Old §§21.032/21.033(a)(1) citations — recodified to §§25.085/25.086 in 1995.
- TEA's 1981–1986 position that homeschooling was illegal — void after *Leeper*.
- Criminal truancy (§25.094) — repealed effective 9/1/2015; truancy is now civil.
- Claims of TEA notification, curriculum approval, 600-minutes/180-day minimums, or grade-level testing — none exist.
- The 1995 Moses commissioner memo — superseded by the 2019 Morath letter.

## Recommended records (not legally required)

- Attendance log
- Portfolio of work samples
- Copy of the withdrawal letter and any district correspondence

## Honest limits

This appendix states public legal facts with pointers to official sources. It is **not legal advice**. "Bona fide" has no statutory definition beyond "good faith, not a sham" — unschooling and purely oral instruction are untested against the written-materials element. For edge cases or disputes, the user should check with their school district or an attorney.
