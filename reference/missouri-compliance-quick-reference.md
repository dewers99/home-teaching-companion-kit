# Missouri home-education quick reference

**Version:** 1.0.1
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

*This appendix holds the verified legal facts the companion consults when the user asks about Missouri home-education requirements. It is consulted on request — never volunteered in full unprompted. Facts verified 2026-09-27. This is public legal information, not legal advice.*

## Governing law

- **RSMo 167.012** — "Home school defined — regular instruction, requirements — daily log" (created by 2024 legislation, effective August 28, 2024; amended by 2025 S.B. 63, effective August 28, 2025). Compulsory attendance: **RSMo 167.031**.
- **Missouri restructured its entire framework in 2024–2025.** Nearly all third-party guides still describe the pre-2024 structure: guides citing "167.031(2)," the voluntary declaration of enrollment, or 167.071 are stale.
- Related: **RSMo 167.013** (FPE school — for ESA/MOScholars families; a different category from home school); **RSMo 167.790** (2025 — home-school/FPE students' access to district activities).
- State authority: Missouri DESE.

## Key definitions

- Compulsory attendance: **ages 7 to 17** — or until 16 credits toward graduation are completed (a "completed credit" = 100+ hours of instruction in a course).
- A "home school" **does not enroll children who participate in the MOScholars/ESA program** — ESA families are FPE schools under 167.013, not home schools.

## The 1,000-hour rule (the operative academic requirement)

The statute names no general subject list. Instead: offer at least **1,000 hours of instruction** per year, at least **600 hours** of which are in **reading, language arts, mathematics, social studies, science, or academic courses related to those areas** and consonant with the child's age and ability — with at least **400 of the 600 hours at the regular home school location**. The records-and-hours requirements **do not apply to any pupil 16 or older**.

## Notification procedure

- **None — no notice to the district, the state, or the county.** The old voluntary declaration of enrollment (1986–2025) was **repealed** by 2025 S.B. 63. DESE: "There is no registration required with the state."
- Withdrawing from public school: a written request to be dropped from the rolls (the only surviving 167.042 function).

## Testing

None.

## Records (required — kept by the parent, filed with no one)

1. A **plan book, diary, or other written record** indicating subjects taught and activities engaged in.
2. A **portfolio of samples** of the child's academic work.
3. A **record of evaluations** of the child's academic progress — or other written/credible evidence equivalent to these.
- **Enforcement is prosecutorial only:** records are "subject to review **only by the local prosecuting attorney**" — not the district, not DESE.
- A **daily log** showing a compliant course of instruction is a **defense to any prosecution** under 167.031 or educational-neglect charges. (Guides insisting on daily hour-logging as a legal mandate overstate the text — the log is an affirmative defense, not a standalone mandate.)

## Teacher qualifications

None.

## Graduation & diplomas; A+ Scholarship

- No state diploma; the parent determines completion and issues the family's diploma.
- **A+ Scholarship: homeschool graduates are NOT eligible** — it requires attending and graduating from a designated A+ high school.

## Re-enrolling in public school

No statute mandates credit acceptance; the decision falls to local school boards (per DESE, under §171.011 — not primary-verified; hedge). RSMo 167.790 (2025) gives home-school and FPE-school students access to district events/activities — new law, no track record yet.

## Outdated-guide warnings

- "File a declaration of enrollment" — repealed; no declaration mechanism exists.
- "The home-school law is at 167.031(2)" — moved to 167.012 in 2024.
- 167.071 citations — repealed 2024.
- "Missouri requires daily hour logs" — overstated; the log is a defense, not a mandate.
- "A+ Scholarship is available to homeschoolers" — false.
- "Umbrella school" requirements — no such concept in Missouri law.
- "Declaration of intent must be filed within 30 days" — false; no filing of any kind exists.

## Honest limits

This appendix states public legal facts with pointers to official sources. It is **not legal advice**. Untested in the research: whether the Children's Division can compel records in a child-welfare investigation; what counts as "equivalent evidence" to the three record categories (never defined by a court — relying on the alternative carries legal risk); how the 1,000-hour rule is practically measured; the scope of 167.790 in practice. For edge cases or disputes, the user should check with their school district or an attorney.
