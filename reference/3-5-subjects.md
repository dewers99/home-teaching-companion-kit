# 3–5 subject reference (3rd through 5th grade)

**Version:** 1.0.1

*This appendix holds the typical subject structure and grade-level objectives for 3rd through 5th grade (roughly ages 8–11). Consulted when the parent asks what to teach, in what order, or whether their child is on track — never volunteered in full unprompted. Objectives are typical ranges, not deadlines; children routinely work across grade levels in different subjects. Content below is typical U.S. scope and sequence, calibrated against Ohio's learning standards as a worked example. It is planning help, not legal requirements — the family's state compliance module lists what their state actually requires, and that's the binding document.*

## How the companion uses this appendix

- Work **subject by subject**, grade by grade. Start from what the child can do today, not the grade label.
- The big shift in 3–5: the child moves from **learning to read → reading to learn**. Content subjects (science, history) become real, daily-or-weekly work rather than light touch.
- **Independent work grows**: by 5th grade a child should handle meaningful stretches of independent reading, writing, and practice — freeing the parent to work with younger siblings or just breathe.
- A rough weekly rhythm: **2–3 hours of focused work per day** in 3rd, growing toward **3–4 hours** by 5th. Language arts and math still anchor the day; science and social studies each get 2–3 sessions per week; art, music, and movement continue woven through.
- If the family chose faith integration at intake, Bible study can deepen here (reading passages independently, memory work, discussion) — still opt-in, still shaped by the family's choice.

## English language arts

**3rd grade**
- Reads chapter books with comprehension; uses strategies like predicting, questioning, and summarizing
- Cursive handwriting is commonly introduced here (print fluency first; either order is fine)
- Grammar: parts of speech (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs), sentence types, subject/predicate
- Spelling: patterns and rules (not just memorized lists)
- Composition: paragraphs with topic sentences; copywork and dictation as training wheels

**4th grade**
- Reads longer novels; identifies theme, character motivation, plot structure
- Grammar: prepositions, conjunctions, possessives; correct punctuation in dialogue and complex sentences
- Vocabulary becomes its own strand (roots, prefixes, suffixes, context clues)
- Composition: multi-paragraph pieces — narratives, book reports, simple research summaries; the writing process (draft → revise → edit)

**5th grade**
- Reads across genres with analysis: compares texts, supports opinions with evidence from the book
- Grammar: clauses, verb tenses, agreement; diagramming optional but useful for some learners
- Composition: structured essays (introduction, body, conclusion); creative writing; note-taking and outlining from sources
- Independent reading is substantial — 30+ minutes daily of the child's own choosing counts fully

## Mathematics

**3rd grade**
- Multiplication and division facts to 10 × 10 — the year's central mission; everything after depends on it
- Fractions as parts of a whole: halves through eighths on number lines and shapes
- Multi-digit addition and subtraction with regrouping; rounding and estimation
- Area and perimeter of rectangles; telling time; bar graphs

**4th grade**
- Multi-digit multiplication (up to 4-digit × 1-digit, 2-digit × 2-digit) and long division with single-digit divisors
- Fraction equivalence, ordering, and simple addition/subtraction of like fractions
- Decimals introduced (tenths, hundredths) and connected to fractions
- Protractor work: angles; classifying triangles and quadrilaterals; line plots

**5th grade**
- Fraction operations: addition/subtraction with unlike denominators, multiplication and division of fractions
- Decimal operations: all four operations with decimals; connecting decimals, fractions, and percents
- Volume of rectangular prisms; coordinate plane (first quadrant)
- Order of operations; numerical expressions; pre-algebra readiness (patterns, variables as unknowns)

## Science

Many families rotate one branch per year across 3–5 (life → earth → physical); any order works.

- **Life science:** ecosystems and food webs, plant and animal life cycles, adaptations, the human body systems
- **Earth science:** rock cycle, weathering and erosion, water cycle, weather vs. climate, the solar system
- **Physical science:** states of matter, simple machines, energy (light, sound, heat), magnets and electricity basics
- Throughout: the **scientific method in practice** — question, hypothesis, experiment, observation, conclusion — via simple hands-on investigations, not worksheets about the method

## History, government & social studies

The classic upper-elementary arc, and it maps cleanly onto typical state subject areas:

- **3rd grade — Communities:** local community and its history, map skills (using maps, not just looking at them), cultures and traditions, basic economics (goods, services, producers, consumers)
- **4th grade — State history:** the natural year for **your state's history** — the state's geography, Native peoples, settlement, statehood, government, and economy. (Ohio's academic standards place state history in 4th grade, and most states do the same.)
- **5th grade — U.S. history:** early America through the Civil War era — exploration, colonization, the Revolution, the Constitution, westward expansion
- **Government strand (woven through):** the three branches, the Constitution and Bill of Rights, citizenship and civic responsibility, how elections work — deepening each year
- Timelines, historical fiction read-alouds, and local field trips (historical sites, museums, the statehouse) carry more weight than any textbook

## Art, music & physical activity

Still woven through, but can take slightly more structured form if the child shows interest:

- Art: drawing instruction, art history picture study, crafts tied to history/science units
- Music: instrument lessons if the child wants them; choir; continued listening and singing
- Movement: daily active play plus organized sports or lessons as the family chooses — PE remains life, not a subject

## Study skills (emerging in 4th–5th)

- Using a planner or simple checklist to track assignments
- Note-taking from reading; outlining before writing
- Test-taking basics: reading directions, managing time, showing work
- Beginning keyboarding/typing practice

## Honest limits

This appendix reflects widely used homeschool scope-and-sequence patterns and public academic standards — not a certified curriculum and not an assessment tool. Grade labels are conveniences. If the parent asks about alignment to a specific state standard, only claim what can honestly be checked against the actual standard.
