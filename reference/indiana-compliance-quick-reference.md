# Indiana home-education quick reference

**Version:** 1.0.1
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

*This appendix holds the verified legal facts the companion consults when the user asks about Indiana home-education requirements. It is consulted on request — never volunteered in full unprompted. Facts verified 2026-09-27. This is public legal information, not legal advice.*

## Governing law

- Indiana has **no "homeschool statute."** Homeschools operate as **nonaccredited nonpublic schools** under the compulsory-attendance chapter, **IC 20-33-2** (key sections 20-33-2-6, -12, -20, -21, -27/-28, -28.6).
- **HEA 1348-2025** (IC 20-32-4-20, effective **July 1, 2025**): a diploma/credential from a nonaccredited nonpublic school is legally sufficient proof of high-school completion statewide; agencies and schools may not treat a person differently based solely on its source.
- State authority: Indiana Department of Education (IDOE).

## Key definitions

- Compulsory attendance ages: **7–18**. Kindergarten is not mandatory.
- Ages 16–18 may exit via the exit-interview withdrawal process.

## Required subjects

**None named.** Nonaccredited nonpublic schools are exempt from all state curriculum and program requirements. The operative duty: the child must receive "instruction equivalent to that given in the public schools" — a phrase state law does not define.

## Notification procedure

- **None required.** IDOE: "Reporting homeschool enrollment (registering a homeschool) is not required in Indiana." A voluntary IDOE online enrollment form exists (no deadline). Enrollment counts are provided only on request of the state superintendent.
- **Leaving public school:** notify the principal **in writing**.
- **High-school students** moving to homeschool must complete the state board's signed **"Withdrawal to Non-Accredited Nonpublic School"** form (IC 20-33-2-28.6). **Without it, the student is treated as a dropout and the BMV revokes or refuses a learner's permit or license.** This is the one Indiana deadline that bites.

## Testing

None. IDOE: "State law does not require state testing, or any other testing, for children in homeschools." (Narrow exception: public-school sports eligibility for homeschoolers can include IDOE-authorized exams — a sports rule, not a homeschooling mandate.)

## Records

**Attendance only.** Maintain an accurate daily record of attendance and produce it **on request** of the state or local superintendent (no special form required). IDOE: **180 days** of instruction per year; the parent chooses days and hours; prior-school days count for mid-year transfers.

## Teacher qualifications

None.

## Graduation & diplomas

Parent-determined: the home school sets its own graduation requirements and issues the diploma. Since July 2025 the diploma is legally sufficient statewide, and the school's administrator may execute documents evidencing enrollment, grades, and transcripts. Related: homeschoolers may take the HSE test at 16+; nonpublic nonaccredited students are not eligible for 21st Century Scholars.

## Re-enrolling in public school

Placement and credit are up to the receiving school: age-appropriate or level-based placement, achievement testing, credit-accumulation alignment, or a committee (including end-of-course exams). Parents may submit test results, attendance records, and curriculum.

## Outdated-guide warnings

- "Compulsory age 7–16" — outdated; it's 7–18.
- "You must register / get a homeschool number" — false.
- SB 483 (2025) would have required curriculum plans and superintendent meetings — **died in committee; not law.**
- Work permits were eliminated July 1, 2021 — old guides are stale.
- "Attendance records reported to the state" — misstatement; kept and produced on request, not filed.
- Pre-July-2025 guides doubting nonaccredited diplomas miss HEA 1348.

## Recommended records (beyond the mandated attendance log)

- Portfolio of work samples across subjects
- Copies of the public-school withdrawal letter/form and any district correspondence
