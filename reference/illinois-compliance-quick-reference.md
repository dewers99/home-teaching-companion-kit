# Illinois home-education quick reference

**Version:** 1.0.1
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

*This appendix holds the verified legal facts the companion consults when the user asks about Illinois home-education requirements. It is consulted on request — never volunteered in full unprompted. Facts verified 2026-09-27. This is public legal information, not legal advice.*

## Governing law

- Illinois has **no "homeschool statute."** Homeschools operate as **private schools** under the compulsory-attendance exemption in **105 ILCS 5/26-1**.
- ***People v. Levisen* (1950)** (Illinois Supreme Court): home schooling counts as private schooling "if the teacher were competent, the required subjects were taught, and the student received an education at least equivalent to public schooling." ***Scoma v. Chicago Board of Education* (1974)** placed the **burden of proof on the parents** to establish their program meets state requirements.
- Illinois has the lightest regulatory touch of all nine kit states.
- State authority: Illinois State Board of Education (ISBE).

## Key definitions

- Compulsory attendance: **ages 6 (on or before September 1) to 17** (since the 2014–2015 school year; older guides citing 7–16 or 7–17 are outdated).

## Required subjects

Instruction must be provided **in the English language** in: **Language Arts; Mathematics; Biological and Physical Science; Social Science (Social Studies); Fine Arts; and Physical Development and Health** — and the education must be **at least equivalent** to what public schools offer. (The statutory branches list is commonly cited as 105 ILCS 5/27-1, but that citation could not be primary-verified on 2026-09-27; the subject list itself is confirmed via ISBE.)

## Notification procedure

- **None required.** ISBE verbatim: "The state of Illinois does not require parents to register before they begin home schooling." ISBE offers a **voluntary** registration form — "There are no other forms, documents, or procedures required by the state of Illinois." Guides describing the voluntary form as mandatory are misleading.
- **Leaving public school:** no formal withdrawal procedure is mandated, but ISBE **highly recommends** notifying the school with a dated letter (keep copies), enclosing the voluntary registration form, and sending the same to the Regional Office of Education. Skipping this risks the school marking the child absent and referring the case to a truancy officer.

## Testing

None — and any tests the parent chooses to give need not be submitted to anyone.

## Records

None mandated by statute. **But the burden of proof matters:** the regional superintendent investigates noncompliance reports and "may expect the parents… to establish, when necessary, that they are providing instruction that is at least commensurate with the standards established for public schools." A program that doesn't meet the standard can draw a truancy referral and a **Class C misdemeanor**. Keep an attendance log and a portfolio of work samples — they're the parent's proof.

## Teacher qualifications

None.

## Graduation & diplomas; college

- **Parent-determined** — no state diploma mechanism for homeschoolers (the working rule, resting on the statutory framework rather than a primary ISBE statement).
- University of Illinois: "Does Illinois admit homeschooled graduates? **Yes, we encourage you to apply.**" Roughly 60–70 homeschooled students admitted each year, reviewed individually; **no GED required for domestic applicants** (international homeschooled applicants must submit one).

## Re-enrolling in public school

The school determines grade placement — it may test the student or ask for proof of material covered. Districts **may not** require the program to be "registered" or "recognized" by the State Board, and may not make unreasonable or arbitrary placement decisions.

## Outdated-guide warnings

- "You must register" — false; registration is voluntary.
- "Testing is required / scores must be submitted" — false.
- "The district must approve your curriculum" — false; ISBE: parents are "free to decide the manner, time and materials."
- "Compulsory age is 7–16" — outdated; 6 to 17 since 2014–15.
- **"HB 2827 (the Homeschool Act) is law"** — false. It is **not law** but also not fully dead: it passed a House committee in March 2025, was parked in the Rules Committee in April 2025, and the 104th General Assembly runs through 2026, so it could theoretically revive. It would have required an annual Homeschool Declaration to the regional office, a high-school-diploma minimum for the homeschool administrator, and portfolio production on a truant officer's request. **Re-check before the kit publishes** — present as "watch this space," never as current law.

## Recommended records (not legally required, but they're the parent's proof)

- Attendance log
- Portfolio of work samples across subjects
- Copies of the public-school withdrawal letter and voluntary registration form
