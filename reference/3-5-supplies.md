# 3–5 supplies & materials reference

**Version:** 1.0.1

*This appendix holds the recommended supplies and materials for 3rd through 5th grade (roughly ages 8–11), organized so the companion can build a tailored checklist or shopping list for any budget. Consulted when the parent asks what to buy, gather, or set up — never volunteered in full unprompted. Same three-path format: buy it, a budget tip, and a household alternative. Assumes the family owns the K–2 basics — this list is what gets added or upgraded.*

## How the companion uses this appendix

- Ask about **budget** before recommending: tight, moderate, or "buy the good stuff." Never assume.
- Start from what the family **already owns** — most 3–5 needs are organization and subject-specific tools, not a fresh start.
- Suggest sourcing in this order: things the family already owns → Buy Nothing / thrift / dollar store → library → buy new.

## Writing & organization (the big upgrade)

| Item (what it's for) | Budget tip | Household alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Notebooks, one per subject (wide ruled → college ruled by 5th) | Back-to-school sales; buy the year's worth at once | Stapled copy-paper booklets |
| Folders or a binder with dividers (per-subject organization) | — | Labeled envelopes, paper-bag pockets |
| Pencils + erasers (consumption increases) | Bulk boxes | — |
| Pens, blue/black (5th grade drafting and editing) | — | — |
| Highlighters, multi-color (finding key words, text evidence) | One 4-pack | — |
| Sticky notes (marking books, reminders) | Dollar store | Small cut paper + tape |
| Index cards, lined (vocabulary, states/capitals, review) | — | Cut-up cereal boxes or cardstock scraps |
| Planner or simple assignment checklist (4th–5th independence) | — | A notebook page ruled by hand; a printed weekly template |

## Math tools

| Item (what it's for) | Budget tip | Household alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Multiplication/division flashcards (3rd grade fact fluency) | — | Handmade on index cards — making them is itself practice |
| Ruler, protractor, compass (4th–5th geometry) | One inexpensive geometry set | A printed paper protractor works in a pinch |
| Graph paper (multi-digit operations, coordinate plane) | — | Print free graph paper; the library printer counts |
| Basic calculator (5th grade: checking work, not replacing it) | Dollar store | Phone calculator with supervision |

## Notes for the homeschool (not classroom) context

- Skip classroom-communal bulk items — at home those are household supplies.
- Buy **per child**, and only for the term ahead. A 3rd grader's needs in September differ from May.
- **Multiplication flashcards and index cards** are the two highest-leverage cheap purchases in this band.

## Safety notes

- Compass points are sharp — supervise, and store with younger siblings in mind.
- Standard art materials should be non-toxic; check age labels.

## Honest limits

This appendix reflects commonly recommended 3–5 materials and widely shared budget practices — not professional safety advice. Age labels on commercial products exist for a reason; when in doubt, follow them.
