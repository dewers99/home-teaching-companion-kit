# K–2 subject reference (Kindergarten through 2nd grade)

**Version:** 1.0.1

*This appendix holds the typical subject structure and grade-level objectives for Kindergarten through 2nd grade (roughly ages 5–8). Unlike Pre-K — which is organized by developmental domain (see `reference/prek-domains.md`) — K–2 is organized by subject, because formal instruction in subject areas begins here. Consulted when the parent asks what to teach, in what order, or whether their child is on track — never volunteered in full unprompted. Objectives are typical ranges, not deadlines; children vary widely, especially in reading. Content below is typical U.S. scope and sequence, calibrated against Ohio's learning standards as a worked example. It is planning help, not legal requirements — the family's state compliance module lists what their state actually requires, and that's the binding document.*

## How the companion uses this appendix

- Work **subject by subject**, grade by grade. If the parent is unsure of the child's level, start from what the child can do today, not the grade label.
- **Reading and math carry the weight** in K–2 — they get the most time and the most patience. Everything else is lighter touch at this age and that's normal.
- A rough weekly rhythm for K: about **1–2 hours of focused seatwork per day** (10–20 minute lessons), with language arts the heaviest (~5–6 hrs/week), math next (~2.5–3 hrs), science and social studies ~1 hour each, and art/music/movement woven through the day rather than scheduled as formal blocks. Time grows gradually through 1st and 2nd.
- If the family chose faith integration at intake, Bible reading and memory work sit naturally inside language arts (reading) and the weekly rhythm — never as a separate forced subject unless the family wants it that way.

## English language arts

The core of K–2. Three strands that braid together: learning to read, learning to write, and being read to.

**Kindergarten**
- Names all 26 uppercase and lowercase letters; produces the primary sound for each consonant and the short-vowel sounds
- Hears and produces rhymes; blends and segments simple sounds (c-a-t → cat)
- Reads simple consonant-vowel-consonant words (cat, mop, sit)
- Holds a pencil correctly; writes letters and own name
- Listens to stories read aloud daily; retells and answers questions

**1st grade**
- Masters consonant digraphs (sh, ch, th), blends (bl, cr, st), long-vowel patterns, silent e
- Reads grade-level text with growing fluency; uses context and phonics to tackle new words
- Spells common phonetic words; begins conventional spelling of high-frequency words
- Writes complete sentences with capital letters and ending punctuation
- Reads independently for short stretches; reads aloud with expression

**2nd grade**
- Reads fluently with comprehension; tackles multi-syllable words using prefixes, suffixes, and root clues
- Spelling becomes systematic (patterns, word families, common irregulars)
- Grammar basics: nouns, verbs, adjectives; subject-verb agreement in simple sentences
- Composition via copywork and dictation; writes short paragraphs with a beginning, middle, and end
- Independent reading across subjects — science and social studies books count as reading practice

## Mathematics

Concrete before abstract at every step — manipulatives first, symbols second.

**Kindergarten**
- Counts to 100 by ones and tens; counts forward from any number, not just 1
- Reads, writes, and counts with numerals 0–20; one-to-one correspondence to 20
- Compares groups (greater than / less than / equal) to 10
- Adds and subtracts within 10 using objects, fingers, or drawings; breaks numbers to 10 into pairs (5 = 2 + 3)
- Names 2-D shapes (circle, square, triangle, rectangle) and 3-D shapes (sphere, cube, cone); sorts by attribute; copies simple patterns

**1st grade**
- Counts to 200; reads and writes numbers to 120
- Adds and subtracts to 20 fluently; two-digit addition and subtraction without regrouping
- Tells time to the hour and half-hour; identifies coins and their values; counts mixed coins
- Measures with nonstandard then standard units (inches, centimeters); compares lengths
- Simple fractions as parts of a whole (halves, quarters); reads basic graphs and charts

**2nd grade**
- Reads, writes, and compares numbers to 1,000; understands hundreds/tens/ones place value
- Adds and subtracts two- and three-digit numbers with carrying and borrowing
- Knows addition and subtraction facts fluently; introduces multiplication as repeated addition
- Measures to the quarter-inch; estimates and rounds; finds area with unit squares
- Identifies even and odd; works with money (making change); reads directions (N/S/E/W) on simple maps

## Science

Light and wonder-driven in K–2 — observation and vocabulary, not memorization.

**Kindergarten–1st**
- The five senses and how we learn through them
- Living vs. nonliving; basic needs of plants and animals
- Weather and seasons: observing, describing, recording
- Plants: parts, what they need, growing things from seeds

**2nd grade**
- Animal groups and habitats; life cycles (frog, butterfly, plant)
- Caring for living things (pets, plants)
- Physical properties of matter: solid/liquid/gas, sink/float, magnets
- Earth basics: rocks, soil, water

## History, government & social studies

At this age these three subject areas blend into one gentle strand: the child, the family, the community, the country.

**Kindergarten–2nd (progressing in depth)**
- Self and family: names, address, family members and roles
- Community helpers and what they do; rules and why they exist (the seed of government)
- Maps and globes: continents, oceans, the child's own state and country; simple map directions
- American symbols, holidays, and their stories (flag, Pledge of Allegiance, Thanksgiving, Independence Day)
- Time vocabulary: past/present/future, yesterday/today/tomorrow, timelines of the child's own life
- 2nd grade: simple biographies of historical figures; beginning map skills (compass rose, map key)

*State-module note: some states' notification or assurance covers required subject areas from compulsory age — check the family's state module for what theirs requires. At K–2, a light, consistent touch — read-alouds, conversations, calendar time, community outings — genuinely counts as instruction in history, government, and social studies. No separate textbook is needed unless the family wants one.*

## Handwriting

Often its own small daily practice in K–2, then fades into writing generally.

- **K:** letter formation (print), proper grip and posture, 5–10 minutes
- **1st:** consistent print; spacing, sizing, and alignment on lines
- **2nd:** fluent print; many families introduce cursive here or in 3rd — either timing is fine. (Context: Ohio's public schools work toward legible print by 3rd grade and legible cursive by 5th grade under House Bill 58; the homeschool statute doesn't mandate handwriting at all, but it's useful context if the child might re-enroll.)

## Art, music & physical activity

Not formal subjects in K–2 — woven through the week.

- Art: drawing, painting, crafts, picture study (looking at real artwork together)
- Music: singing, rhythm, listening to varied music; an instrument only if the child asks
- Movement: daily active play; playground, ball games, bike, swimming — PE is life at this age

## Honest limits

This appendix reflects widely used homeschool scope-and-sequence patterns and public academic standards — not a certified curriculum and not an assessment tool. Grade labels are conveniences; a child working across grade levels in different subjects is completely normal. If the parent asks about alignment to a specific state standard, only claim what can honestly be checked against the actual standard.
