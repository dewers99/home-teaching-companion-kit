# New York home-instruction quick reference

**Version:** 1.0.1
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

*This appendix holds the verified legal facts the companion consults when the user asks about New York home-education requirements. It is consulted on request — never volunteered in full unprompted. Facts verified 2026-09-27. This is public legal information, not legal advice.*

## Governing law

- New York has **no "homeschool statute."** Home instruction is governed by **Commissioner's Regulation 8 NYCRR §100.10**, under the Education Law's compulsory-education provisions.
- The district's role is to be *assured* the child receives instruction in the required subjects — a **compliance-review system, not an approval system**. NYSED's Home Instruction Q&A page is the single best myth-corrector.
- State authority: NYSED.

## Key definitions

- Compulsory attendance: **ages 6–16 statewide; 17 in New York City.** (The NYC 17 rule is confirmed by NYSED; its exact statutory mechanism was not primary-verified.)
- Instructional time: the substantial equivalent of **180 days/year** — **900 hours (grades 1–6), 990 hours (grades 7–12)**.

## Required subjects (per §100.10(e))

- **Grades 1–6:** arithmetic, reading, spelling, writing, English language, geography, U.S. history, science, health education, music, visual arts, physical education, bilingual/ESL where indicated.
- **Grades 7–8:** English, history and geography, science, mathematics, PE, health education, art, music, practical arts, library skills.
- **Grades 9–12:** English 4 units; social studies 4 units (American history 1, government 1/2, economics 1/2); math 2; science 2; art and/or music 1; health 1/2; PE 2; electives 3.
- **All grades:** patriotism and citizenship; alcohol/drug/tobacco health education; highway/bicycle safety; fire and arson prevention. AIDS instruction once each in K–6, 7–8, and 9–12. **PE required at every level.**
- Course content may differ from the public-school curriculum — only the required subjects must be taught.

## The annual paperwork cycle

1. **Notice of intent** — written, to the superintendent of the district of residence, **by July 1** each year (or within 14 days if starting mid-year).
2. **IHIP (Individualized Home Instruction Plan)** — due **within 4 weeks** of receiving the district's materials **or by August 15, whichever is later.** Contents: child's name, age, grade level; syllabi/curriculum materials/textbooks/plan of instruction per subject; quarterly-report dates; instructors' names; statement of meeting Ed Law §3205. District responds within 10 business days or by August 31; deficiencies get written notice; parent revises within 15 days or by September 15. **No board approval required.** Districts may require only §100.10(d)'s listed items. Parents are not required to meet with officials; no health or fire inspections; "socialization" concerns are not grounds for a deficiency finding.
3. **Quarterly reports (four per year)** — hours of instruction; material covered per subject; a grade per subject or a written narrative; written explanation if less than 80% of planned material was covered in any subject.
4. **Annual assessment** — filed with the fourth quarterly report. Default: a commercially published **norm-referenced achievement test** chosen by the parent. Alternative: a **written narrative** from someone who interviewed the child and reviewed a portfolio (certified teacher, peer review panel, or another person chosen by the parent with the superintendent's consent). Grade bands: **1–3:** narrative every year; **4–8:** narrative no more than every other year; **9–12:** test every year, no narrative option. Probation only below the **33rd percentile** composite (or no year of growth), or an inadequate-progress narrative — per child, not per family. Home visits only on probation, on three days' written notice.

## Records

Parents **must keep attendance records**; no other records are legally required. (NYSED recommends keeping evidence of the program, the child's achievement, and district correspondence.)

## Teacher qualifications

None. Parents may use tutors — but if a majority of the program becomes group instruction organized across families, it's operating as a religious or independent school, not home instruction.

## Graduation & diplomas; college

- **No state diploma for home-instructed students.** Parents mirroring public-school requirements are pointed to CR §100.5 (Regents diploma requirements).
- HSE: New York's equivalency exam is now the **GED (TASC replaced January 2022)**; 17- and 18-year-olds home schooled under §100.10 qualify, as do 16-year-olds at maximum compulsory age.
- **SUNY/CUNY — the superintendent's letter:** SUNY requires a **letter from the district superintendent stating the home instruction is substantially equivalent** to the local high school program (ability-to-benefit tests not accepted). CUNY requires a homeschool transcript **plus** the letter (GED without it) — confirm the CUNY detail with CUNY; it was not primary-verified. Districts are **not obligated** to issue the letter, though most do — start this conversation early if SUNY/CUNY is on the horizon. TAP aid requires a diploma, HSE, homeschool documentation, or an ability-to-benefit exam.

## Re-enrolling in public school

Grade placement is the **principal's** decision; credit is at the receiving school's discretion (assessment or evidence of coursework). Summer school is open to all district residents.

## Outdated-guide warnings

- "The district must approve your homeschooling" — false; compliance review, not approval.
- "Standardized testing every year" — false; narrative alternative available in lower grades.
- "You must register your child in the public school" — false.
- "Health and fire inspection reports required" — false.
- Guides naming TASC — stale; GED since January 2022.
- "IHIP due August 15" — incomplete; four weeks after district materials *or* August 15, whichever is later.
- COVID-era waivers were temporary — not current rules.
