# Michigan home-education quick reference

**Version:** 1.0.1
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

*This appendix holds the verified legal facts the companion consults when the user asks about Michigan home-education requirements. It is consulted on request — never volunteered in full unprompted. Facts verified 2026-09-27. This is public legal information, not legal advice.*

## Governing law

- Michigan Compiled Laws **380.1561** (Revised School Code, compulsory school attendance; last amended by 2016 Act 532, effective April 9, 2017).
- Two legal routes — the family may use either or both (380.1561(4)):
  - **(3)(f) — home school:** the child "being educated at the child's home by his or her parent or legal guardian in an organized educational program."
  - **(3)(a) — nonpublic school:** the child attends a state-approved nonpublic school teaching subjects comparable to local public schools (under the Private, Denominational and Parochial Schools Act, 1921 PA 302).
- State authority: Michigan Department of Education (MDE) — which "plays no role with the home school family" under route (f).

## Key definitions

- Compulsory attendance ages: **6–18**. (At 16+, the child may stop with the parent's written notice to resident-district officials.)
- There is no such section as "MCL 388.553a" — guides citing it are citing a nonexistent statute.

## Required subjects

- Route (f) — nine named subjects: reading, spelling, mathematics, science, history, civics, literature, writing, and English grammar. No curriculum, hours, or days specified.
- Route (a) — subjects comparable to the local public schools' course of study (MDE: math, reading, English, science, social studies in all grades; U.S. and Michigan constitutions and civil government in high school).

## Notification procedure

- **Route (f): none.** No notice to the district, MDE, or anyone else is required. (A courtesy call or note to the local school is recommended to avoid truancy-officer involvement.)
- **Route (a):** the Nonpublic School Membership Report (Form SM-4325) to MDE is requested annually — **voluntary unless** the family seeks special-education or auxiliary services from the public school/ISD. (Current due date: re-check; older MDE materials say October 31.)

## Testing

None under either route. State assessments are available voluntarily through the resident district.

## Records

None legally required under either route. MDE encourages keeping progress records (helps with placement if the child re-enrolls).

## Teacher qualifications

- Route (f): **none** — "There are no minimum qualifications for teachers."
- Route (a): the statute technically requires a teaching certificate — but *People v. DeJonge* (1993) held this unconstitutional as applied to families whose religious convictions bar certified instructors.

## Graduation & diplomas

Parent-determined. Report cards, transcripts, and diplomas are "the responsibility of the nonpublic school and home school family (based on internal standards)." Michigan Merit Curriculum requirements do not apply.

## Re-enrolling in public school

No statute governs it. Credits and placement are "solely determined by the receiving public or nonpublic school," which generally reevaluates the student.

## Outdated-guide warnings

- "Must notify the district or MDE within 10 days" — false under route (f).
- "Bachelor's degree / teaching certificate required" — false under route (f).
- "Annual standardized tests required" — false.
- "Must register as a nonpublic school every year" — misleading; voluntary except for service-seekers.
- 2023–24 registration bills failed; re-check for new filings before acting.

## Recommended records (not legally required)

- Attendance log
- Portfolio of work samples
- Copies of any correspondence with schools or the district

## Honest limits
