# Indiana compliance skill

**Version:** 1.0.1
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

## When to use

Use this module when the parent asks about Indiana home-education requirements: what the law requires, what to file, when the deadlines are, whether they're compliant, or how re-enrollment in public school works. If the parent's state isn't settled yet, or they want the cross-state picture, load `skills/compliance-core/` first. This module shares public legal facts and pointers to official sources only. It never gives legal advice, and for anything edge-case or disputed it tells the parent to check with their school district or an attorney.

## Procedure

1. Determine the situation: starting home education for the first time, leaving a public school (especially a high-schooler), annual planning, or a re-enrollment question. The answer differs by situation, so nail this down first.
2. Consult `reference/indiana-compliance-quick-reference.md` for the exact facts before answering — the module states WHEN to consult it and never volunteers the whole appendix unprompted. Give the parent only the piece they need for their situation.
3. Give the specific answer: what to file, when, where, and what to keep. The core facts to convey (facts researched 2026-09-27; do not embellish):
   - Governing law: Indiana has **no "homeschool statute."** Homeschools operate as **nonaccredited nonpublic schools** under the compulsory-attendance chapter, **IC 20-33-2** (key sections: 20-33-2-6, -12, -20, -21, -27/-28, -28.6). New: **HEA 1348-2025** (IC 20-32-4-20, effective July 1, 2025) makes a diploma from a nonaccredited nonpublic school legally sufficient proof of high-school completion statewide.
   - Compulsory attendance ages: **7–18**. (Kindergarten is not mandatory.)
   - **No notification or registration is required.** IDOE: "Reporting homeschool enrollment (registering a homeschool) is not required in Indiana." A voluntary IDOE online enrollment form exists. Enrollment counts are provided only if the state superintendent requests them — not a standing filing.
   - **Leaving public school:** notify the principal in writing. **High-school students must complete the state board's signed "Withdrawal to Non-Accredited Nonpublic School" form** (IC 20-33-2-28.6) — without it, the student is treated as a dropout and the BMV revokes or refuses a learner's permit or license. This is the one Indiana deadline that bites.
   - **No required subjects are named.** The operative duty: the child must receive "instruction equivalent to that given in the public schools" — a phrase state law does not define.
   - **No testing is required.** (The one narrow exception: homeschoolers playing public-school sports may face IDOE-authorized exams as an athletic-eligibility condition — a sports rule, not a homeschooling mandate.)
   - **Record-keeping: attendance only.** Keep an accurate daily record of attendance and produce it on request of the state or local superintendent (no special form). IDOE: 180 days of instruction per year; the parent chooses days and hours; prior-school days count for mid-year transfers.
   - **No parent qualifications.**
   - Graduation and diplomas: the home school sets its own graduation requirements and issues the diploma. Since July 2025, that diploma is legally sufficient statewide and the school's administrator may execute documents evidencing enrollment, grades, and transcripts. Related notes: homeschoolers may take the HSE test at 16+; nonpublic nonaccredited students are not eligible for 21st Century Scholars.
   - Re-enrolling in public school: placement and credit are up to the receiving school (testing, credit alignment, or a committee with end-of-course exams). Parents may submit test results, attendance records, and curriculum.
4. Close with the honest-limits line (this is public legal information, not legal advice; edge cases go to the district or an attorney) and, where relevant, the myth corrections: "compulsory age 7–16" is outdated (it's 7–18); "you must register / get a homeschool number" is false; SB 483 (2025), which would have required curriculum plans and superintendent meetings, died in committee and is not law; work permits were eliminated in 2021; attendance records are kept and produced on request, not filed with the state; and pre-July-2025 guides doubting nonaccredited diplomas miss HEA 1348.
5. Watch-outs to re-check before the parent acts: the exact IC 20-33-2 section text was cross-checked from secondary sources on 2026-09-27 (the legislature's site was unfetchable that day) — verify against iga.in.gov at build/publication time; and check whether 2026's omnibus education bill (HB 1266, P.L. 159-2026) touched IC 20-33-2.
