# California compliance skill

**Version:** 1.0.0
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

## When to use

Use this module when the parent asks about California home-education requirements: what the law requires, what to file, when the deadlines are, whether they're compliant, or how re-enrollment in public school works. If the parent's state isn't settled yet, or they want the cross-state picture, load `skills/compliance-core/` first. This module shares public legal facts and pointers to official sources only. It never gives legal advice, and for anything edge-case or disputed it tells the parent to check with their school district or an attorney.

## Procedure

1. Determine the situation: starting home education for the first time, filing the annual affidavit, or a re-enrollment question. The answer differs by situation, so nail this down first.
2. Consult `reference/california-compliance-quick-reference.md` for the exact facts before answering — the module states WHEN to consult it and never volunteers the whole appendix unprompted. Give the parent only the piece they need for their situation.
3. Give the specific answer: what to file, when, where, and what to keep. The core facts to convey (facts researched 2026-09-27; do not embellish):
   - Governing law: California has **no "homeschool statute."** Homeschooling is lawful as private schooling — the Court of Appeal held in 2008 that "California statutes permit home schooling as a species of private school education" (*In re Rachel L.*, 168 Cal.App.4th 1146). The dominant route is a **home-based private school via the Private School Affidavit (PSA)** under **EC §§ 33190, 33191, 48222**. (Two other routes exist: a private school satellite program under the same sections, and the credentialed-tutor option under EC 48224. Public charters and public independent study are public-school enrollment, not private homeschooling.)
   - Compulsory attendance ages: **6–18**.
   - **The Private School Affidavit is the one filing that matters.** File it with the Superintendent of Public Instruction (CDE), under penalty of perjury, by the school's owner/head. Statutory window: **"between the first and 15th day of October of each year."** In practice the CDE online system runs **August 1–June 30**, and early filing is valid without October re-filing. Key points: parents are **not** required to report to their school district (districts verify by confirming affidavit filing only when they inquire about a non-enrolled child); filing is **not** state approval or endorsement; a paper "statement in lieu of the affidavit" is permitted.
   - Required subjects: private full-time day schools must teach in English and "offer instruction in **the several branches of study required to be taught in the public schools**" (EC 48222). CDE points families to EC 51210 (grades 1–6) and EC 51220 (grades 7–12) as the state courses of study. Treat those lists as CDE's stated interpretation — how binding they are on private schools was not settled by any enforcement mechanism or case law found in the research, so hedge with "check the official source."
   - **No testing is required.** CDE FAQ verbatim: "While state testing is not required of private schools, parents can test their students" voluntarily.
   - **Records are required — three categories:** (1) an **attendance register** recording clearly every absence of half a day or more; (2) the **courses of study** offered; (3) **faculty names, addresses, and educational qualifications**, maintained at the stated address and attested true and accurate. Keep a copy of the affidavit at the school. (Individual pupil records are only "highly recommended." Guides citing 5 CCR § 11960 are citing charter-school regulations — wrong.)
   - **No parent qualifications** under the PSA route — EC 48222 requires only "persons capable of teaching," which is undefined; no diploma, degree, or credential required. (The credentialed-tutor route under EC 48224 does require a valid state credential, 3 hours/day, 175 days/year, 8 a.m.–4 p.m.) Fingerprinting expressly excludes "a parent or legal guardian working exclusively with his or her children."
   - Graduation and diplomas: **no law or regulation establishes graduation or diploma requirements for California private schools** — the parent creates the diploma and transcripts. CDE cautions a home-school diploma "may not be generally recognized"; GED/California Proficiency Program are alternatives. Note: pupils in home-based private schools are **exempt from school immunization requirements** (H&S Code 120335(f)).
   - Re-enrolling in public school: public school districts set their own placement policies and **may refuse to recognize credits** the parent conferred; the former school must transfer the pupil's permanent record on request.
4. Close with the honest-limits line (this is public legal information, not legal advice; edge cases go to the district or an attorney) and, where relevant, the myth corrections: the February 2008 *Jonathan L.* opinion suggesting credential requirements was depublished and replaced by the August 2008 published opinion — guides citing credential requirements rely on the dead opinion; AB 2756 (2018), with its fire-marshal inspections and new affidavit data, died in committee and is not law; "October 1–15 only" is outdated in practice (CDE accepts August–June); and the U.S. Dept. of Ed.'s state-regulation page (~2016) is stale.
5. Watch-outs to re-check before the parent acts: how binding the 51210/51220 subject lists are on private schools; the legal effect of a late PSA filing; and district verification practice in the parent's area — all flagged as unverified/ambiguous in the 2026-09-27 research.

## Output

Direct answers to the parent's question. If the AI tool cannot save files, say so plainly and provide any checklist as copy-paste text.

## Boundaries

- Never legal advice. If the question goes beyond the verified facts — disputes with a district, special-education questions, truancy proceedings, or anything adversarial — say so honestly and point the parent to their school district or an attorney.
- Never presents the 51210/51220 subject lists as a litigated mandate on private schools, the effect of a late affidavit filing, or district verification practice as settled — all are hedged in the research.
- Defers record-keeping and progress questions to the progress-tracking module; defers curriculum questions to the curriculum-and-resource-guidance module.
