# New York compliance skill

**Version:** 1.0.0
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

## When to use

Use this module when the parent asks about New York home-education requirements: what the law requires, what to file, when the deadlines are, whether they're compliant, or how re-enrollment in public school works. If the parent's state isn't settled yet, or they want the cross-state picture, load `skills/compliance-core/` first. This module shares public legal facts and pointers to official sources only. It never gives legal advice, and for anything edge-case or disputed it tells the parent to check with their school district or an attorney.

## Procedure

1. Determine the situation: starting home instruction for the first time, the annual notice/IHIP cycle, quarterly reports, the annual assessment, or a re-enrollment/college question. The answer differs by situation, so nail this down first. New York is the most paperwork-intensive state in the kit — walk the parent through one step at a time rather than dumping the whole cycle.
2. Consult `reference/new-york-compliance-quick-reference.md` for the exact facts before answering — the module states WHEN to consult it and never volunteers the whole appendix unprompted. Give the parent only the piece they need for their situation.
3. Give the specific answer: what to file, when, where, and what to keep. The core facts to convey (facts researched 2026-09-27; do not embellish):
   - Governing law: New York has **no "homeschool statute."** Home instruction is governed by **Commissioner's Regulation 8 NYCRR §100.10**, under the Education Law's compulsory-education provisions. The district's role is to be *assured* the child receives instruction in the required subjects — a **compliance-review system, not an approval system**. (NYSED's primary Q&A page is the single best myth-corrector in the state.)
   - Compulsory attendance: **ages 6–16 statewide; 17 in New York City.** (The NYC 17 rule is confirmed by NYSED; its exact statutory mechanism was not primary-verified — hedge if it matters.)
   - **The annual paperwork cycle:**
     - **Notice of intent** — written, to the superintendent of the district of residence, **by July 1** each school year (or within 14 days if starting mid-year).
     - **IHIP (Individualized Home Instruction Plan)** — due **within 4 weeks** of receiving the district's materials **or by August 15, whichever is later.** Contents: child's name, age, grade level; syllabi, curriculum materials, textbooks, or plan of instruction for each required subject; quarterly-report dates; instructors' names; statement of meeting Ed Law §3205. The district must respond within 10 business days or by August 31; a deficient IHIP gets written notice of the deficiency and the parent revises within 15 days or by September 15. **No board approval is required.** Districts may require only the information §100.10(d) lists — nothing more. Parents are not required to meet with officials, and "socialization" concerns are explicitly not grounds for a deficiency finding.
     - **Quarterly reports (four per year)** — on the parent-chosen IHIP dates: hours of instruction that quarter; material covered in each subject; a grade in each subject or a written narrative; a written explanation if less than 80% of planned material was covered in any subject.
     - **Annual assessment** — filed with the fourth quarterly report. Default: a commercially published **norm-referenced achievement test** chosen by the parent. Alternative: a **written narrative** from someone who interviewed the child and reviewed a portfolio — a certified teacher, a peer review panel, or another person chosen by the parent with the superintendent's consent. Grade bands: **grades 1–3:** narrative usable every year; **grades 4–8:** narrative no more than every other year (test in the alternate years); **grades 9–12:** standardized test every year, no narrative option. Probation only if the test composite is **below the 33rd percentile** (or shows no year of growth) or the narrative certifies inadequate progress — per child, not per family. A district may require a home visit **only when the program is on probation**, on three days' written notice.
   - **Required subjects** (per §100.10(e)): **grades 1–6** — arithmetic, reading, spelling, writing, English language, geography, U.S. history, science, health education, music, visual arts, physical education, bilingual/ESL where indicated; **grades 7–8** — English, history and geography, science, mathematics, PE, health education, art, music, practical arts, library skills; **grades 9–12** — English 4 units, social studies 4 units (American history 1, government 1/2, economics 1/2), math 2, science 2, art and/or music 1, health 1/2, PE 2, electives 3. **All grades:** patriotism and citizenship; alcohol/drug/tobacco health education; highway/bicycle safety; fire and arson prevention. AIDS instruction once each in K–6, 7–8, and 9–12. **PE is required at every level.** Instructional time: the substantial equivalent of **180 days/year** — **900 hours (grades 1–6), 990 hours (grades 7–12)**. Course content may differ from the public-school curriculum — only the required subjects must be taught.
   - **Records:** parents **must keep attendance records**; no other records are legally required. (NYSED recommends keeping evidence of the program, the child's achievement, and district correspondence.)
   - **No parent qualifications.** Parents may use tutors; but if a majority of the program becomes group instruction organized across families, it's operating as a religious or independent school, not home instruction.
   - Graduation and diplomas: **no state diploma for home-instructed students** — a diploma may only go to a student enrolled in a registered secondary school. Parents mirroring public-school requirements are pointed to CR §100.5 (Regents diploma requirements). HSE path: New York's equivalency exam is now the **GED (TASC was replaced in January 2022)**; 17- and 18-year-olds who were home schooled under §100.10 qualify, as do 16-year-olds at maximum compulsory age.
   - **College — the superintendent's letter.** SUNY policy requires homeschooled applicants to community colleges to submit **a letter from the district superintendent stating the home instruction is substantially equivalent** to the local high school program; ability-to-benefit tests are not accepted as a diploma alternative. CUNY requires a homeschool transcript **plus** the superintendent's letter (without it, a GED is required) — note the CUNY detail was not primary-verified on cuny.edu, so flag it as "confirm with CUNY." Districts are **not obligated** to issue the letter, though most do — a genuine practical gap: start this conversation early if SUNY/CUNY is on the horizon. TAP financial aid requires a diploma, HSE, homeschool documentation, or an ability-to-benefit exam.
   - Re-enrolling in public school: grade placement is the **principal's** decision; credit is at the receiving school's discretion (assessment or evidence of coursework). Summer school is open to all district residents.
4. Close with the honest-limits line (this is public legal information, not legal advice; edge cases go to the district or an attorney) and, where relevant, the myth corrections: the district does **not** approve homeschooling (compliance review, not approval); standardized testing is **not** required every year; the child does **not** need to be registered in the public school; no health or fire inspections; any guide naming TASC is stale (GED since 2022); "IHIP due August 15" is incomplete (four weeks after district materials *or* August 15, whichever is later); COVID-era waivers were temporary.
5. Watch-outs to re-check before the parent acts: whether §100.10 gained permanent post-2021 amendments; the NYC age-17 mechanism; the Letter of Substantial Equivalency voluntary-vs-required gap; and **Chapter 606 of the Laws of 2025** (online high-school diplomas/TAP eligibility) — in flux, with a Regents vote expected December 2026.

## Boundaries

- Never legal advice. If the question goes beyond the verified facts — disputes with a district, special-education questions, truancy proceedings, or anything adversarial — say so honestly and point the parent to their school district or an attorney.
- Never presents the CUNY transcript-plus-letter detail as primary-verified; it needs confirmation with CUNY.
- Never presents the NYC age-17 mechanism, kindergarten hour figures, or Chapter 606's final form as settled — all flagged as unverified/in flux in the 2026-09-27 research.
- Defers record-keeping and progress questions to the progress-tracking module; defers curriculum questions to the curriculum-and-resource-guidance module.
