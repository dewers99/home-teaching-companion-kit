# Ohio compliance skill

**Version:** 1.0.1
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

## When to use

Use this module when the parent asks about Ohio home-education requirements: what the law requires, what to file, when the deadlines are, whether they're compliant, or how re-enrollment in public school works. This module shares public legal facts and pointers to official sources only. It never gives legal advice, and for anything edge-case or disputed it tells the parent to check with their school district or an attorney.

## Procedure

1. Determine the situation: starting home education for the first time, annual renewal, mid-year move or withdrawal from a school, or a re-enrollment question. The answer differs by situation, so nail this down first.
2. Consult `reference/ohio-compliance-quick-reference.md` for the exact facts before answering — the module states WHEN to consult it and never volunteers the whole appendix unprompted. Give the parent only the piece they need for their situation.
3. Give the specific answer: what to file, when, where, and what to keep. For a notification walkthrough, use `templates/notification-checklist.template.md` and work through it with the parent, filling the state-specific lines from this module. The core facts to convey (facts researched 2026-09-27; do not embellish):
   - Governing law: Ohio Revised Code 3321.042, effective October 3, 2023 (House Bill 33, 135th General Assembly). Official text at codes.ohio.gov; state authority is the Ohio Department of Education & Workforce.
   - "Home education" is parent-directed education of a child ages 6–18; compulsory attendance ages are 6–18. Under 6: no notification obligation.
   - A child receiving home education in the 6 required subject areas is exempt from compulsory attendance. The 6 areas: English language arts, mathematics, science, history, government, social studies.
   - The notice goes to the superintendent of the district of residence WITHIN 5 CALENDAR DAYS of starting, moving into a new district, or withdrawing from a school, and ANNUALLY BY AUGUST 30 thereafter. It contains ONLY: parent's name and address, child's name, and assurance the child will receive education in the required subject areas. The exemption takes effect immediately upon receipt. The superintendent must send written acknowledgment within 14 calendar days — keep that letter.
   - Re-enrolling in public school: the child is placed in the appropriate grade level without discrimination or prejudice, per district policy.
   - Graduation and diplomas: Ohio law sets no course, credit, testing, or diploma requirements for home-educated graduates — the parent determines when the student has completed their education and issues the diploma. Colleges, employers, and the military set their own admissions expectations; keep the transcript and course descriptions (see `reference/9-12-subjects.md`). Public legal information, not legal advice.
   - ELIMINATED by the October 2023 change — never present as current law: the 900-hour annual minimum, annual assessments or testing or portfolio review, teacher qualification requirements, curriculum outline submission, detailed record-keeping mandates.
   - Record-keeping is not legally mandated, but recommend keeping simple records anyway: copies of each year's notification, the superintendent's acknowledgment letters, and progress logs — useful for re-enrollment, College Credit Plus applications, or personal organization.
4. Close with the honest-limits line (this is public legal information, not legal advice; edge cases go to the district or an attorney) and, where relevant, the outdated-guide warning: a large share of online guides still describe the pre-October-2023 rules — if a guide mentions 900 hours, annual assessments, or teacher qualifications for Ohio, it is outdated; check the guide's date and verify against ORC 3321.042.

State routing: this module is one of nine state compliance modules under `skills/compliance-core/`. When the family hasn't yet settled on a state, or wants the cross-state picture, start at the compliance core; when the state is known, use that state's module (michigan-compliance, indiana-compliance, texas-compliance, california-compliance, florida-compliance, new-york-compliance, missouri-compliance, illinois-compliance, or this one).

## Output

Direct answers to the parent's question plus the filled `templates/notification-checklist.template.md` when walking through a notification. If the AI tool cannot save files, say so plainly and provide the checklist as copy-paste text.

## Boundaries

- Never legal advice. If the question goes beyond the verified facts — disputes with a district, special-education questions, truancy proceedings, or anything adversarial — say so honestly and point the parent to their school district or an attorney.
- Never invents district-specific rules or forms; districts' procedures may differ, and guessing at them is exactly what this module refuses to do.
- Never presents pre-2023 requirements (900 hours, assessments, teacher qualifications) as current law. If the parent quotes one of these, correct it with the October 2023 change and point at ORC 3321.042.
- Defers record-keeping and progress questions to the progress-tracking module; defers curriculum questions to the curriculum-and-resource-guidance module.
