# Michigan compliance skill

**Version:** 1.0.0
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

## When to use

Use this module when the parent asks about Michigan home-education requirements: what the law requires, what to file, when the deadlines are, whether they're compliant, or how re-enrollment in public school works. If the parent's state isn't settled yet, or they want the cross-state picture, load `skills/compliance-core/` first. This module shares public legal facts and pointers to official sources only. It never gives legal advice, and for anything edge-case or disputed it tells the parent to check with their school district or an attorney.

## Procedure

1. Determine the situation: starting home education for the first time, choosing between Michigan's two legal routes, leaving a public school, or a re-enrollment question. The answer differs by situation, so nail this down first.
2. Consult `reference/michigan-compliance-quick-reference.md` for the exact facts before answering — the module states WHEN to consult it and never volunteers the whole appendix unprompted. Give the parent only the piece they need for their situation.
3. Give the specific answer: what to file, when, where, and what to keep. The core facts to convey (facts researched 2026-09-27; do not embellish):
   - Governing law: Michigan Compiled Laws **380.1561** (Revised School Code, compulsory school attendance; last amended by 2016 Act 532, effective April 9, 2017). Michigan offers **two legal routes** and the family may use either or both:
     - **Exemption (f) — home school:** the child "being educated at the child's home by his or her parent or legal guardian in an organized educational program" covering nine named subjects: reading, spelling, mathematics, science, history, civics, literature, writing, and English grammar. **No notice to anyone is required.** The Michigan Department of Education "plays no role with the home school family."
     - **Exemption (a) — nonpublic school:** the child attends a state-approved nonpublic school teaching subjects comparable to the local public schools' course of study (treated under the Private, Denominational and Parochial Schools Act, 1921 PA 302). Reporting to MDE via the annual Nonpublic School Membership Report (Form SM-4325) is **voluntary unless** the family seeks special-education or auxiliary services from the public school.
   - Compulsory attendance ages: **6–18**. (At 16+, the child may stop with the parent's written notice to resident-district officials.)
   - **No testing is required** under either route. **No records are legally required** under either route — though keeping progress records is encouraged (they help with placement if the child re-enrolls).
   - **No parent qualifications** under the home-school route. Under the nonpublic-school route, the statute technically requires a teaching certificate — but *People v. DeJonge* (1993) held that requirement unconstitutional as applied to families whose religious convictions bar certified instructors. This is the one narrow qualifications caveat in all nine kit states; don't let guides turn it into a general rule.
   - Graduation and diplomas: the parent (or nonpublic school) determines completion and issues the diploma based on internal standards. Michigan Merit Curriculum requirements do not apply.
   - Re-enrolling in public school: no statute governs it — credit and placement are solely the receiving school's decision; the school generally reevaluates the child.
   - Record-keeping is not legally mandated, but recommend keeping simple records anyway: an attendance log, a portfolio of work samples, and copies of any correspondence — useful for re-enrollment or personal organization.
4. Close with the honest-limits line (this is public legal information, not legal advice; edge cases go to the district or an attorney) and, where relevant, the myth corrections: many guides say Michigan requires notifying the district or MDE within 10 days (false under the home-school route), a bachelor's degree or teaching certificate (false under the home-school route), annual standardized tests (false), or yearly nonpublic-school registration (misleading — voluntary except for service-seekers). Any guide citing "MCL 388.553a" is citing a section that does not exist.
5. Watch-outs to re-check before the parent acts: the current SM-4325 due date (older MDE materials say October 31; newer guidance suggests an October–November window), and whether any new registration bills have been filed (2023–24 attempts failed).

## Output

Direct answers to the parent's question. If the AI tool cannot save files, say so plainly and provide any checklist as copy-paste text.

## Boundaries

- Never legal advice. If the question goes beyond the verified facts — disputes with a district, special-education questions, truancy proceedings, or anything adversarial — say so honestly and point the parent to their school district or an attorney.
- Never mixes up the two exemptions: the no-notice, no-qualifications home-school route vs. the nonpublic-school route. When a guide's claim sounds burdensome, check which route it's describing — most Michigan myths come from applying route-(a) rules to route-(f) families.
- Defers record-keeping and progress questions to the progress-tracking module; defers curriculum questions to the curriculum-and-resource-guidance module.
