# Missouri compliance skill

**Version:** 1.0.0
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

## When to use

Use this module when the parent asks about Missouri home-education requirements: what the law requires, what to file, when the deadlines are, whether they're compliant, or how re-enrollment in public school works. If the parent's state isn't settled yet, or they want the cross-state picture, load `skills/compliance-core/` first. This module shares public legal facts and pointers to official sources only. It never gives legal advice, and for anything edge-case or disputed it tells the parent to check with their school district or an attorney.

## Procedure

1. Determine the situation: starting home education for the first time, questions about hours and records, ESA/MOScholars participation, or a re-enrollment question. The answer differs by situation, so nail this down first. Also determine whether the family uses ESA/MOScholars funds — those families fall under the **FPE school** category (RSMo 167.013), not the home-school definition in this module.
2. Consult `reference/missouri-compliance-quick-reference.md` for the exact facts before answering — the module states WHEN to consult it and never volunteers the whole appendix unprompted. Give the parent only the piece they need for their situation.
3. Give the specific answer: what to file, when, where, and what to keep. The core facts to convey (facts researched 2026-09-27; do not embellish):
   - Governing law: **RSMo 167.012** — "Home school defined — regular instruction, requirements — daily log" (created by 2024 legislation, effective August 28, 2024; amended by 2025 S.B. 63, effective August 28, 2025). Compulsory attendance is **RSMo 167.031**. **Nearly all third-party guides still describe the pre-2024 structure** — if a guide cites "167.031(2)" for home-school rules, the voluntary declaration of enrollment, or 167.071, it is stale; the law moved, was rewritten, or was repealed.
   - Compulsory attendance: **ages 7 to 17** (or until 16 credits toward graduation are completed — a "completed credit" is 100+ hours of instruction in a course).
   - **No notification or registration of any kind.** The old voluntary declaration of enrollment (1986–2025) was **repealed** by 2025 S.B. 63. DESE: "There is no registration required with the state. There is no program for the inspection, approval, or accreditation of home schools in Missouri." (The only surviving 167.042 function is a written request to be dropped from public-school rolls when withdrawing — a withdrawal request, not a homeschool registration.)
   - **The 1,000-hour rule** (the operative academic requirement; the statute names no general subject list): offer at least **1,000 hours of instruction** per year, at least **600 hours** of which are in **reading, language arts, mathematics, social studies, science, or academic courses related to those areas** and consonant with the child's age and ability — with at least **400 of the 600 hours at the regular home school location**. The records-and-hours requirements **do not apply to any pupil 16 or older**.
   - **Records are required — kept by the parent, filed with no one:** (a) a **plan book, diary, or other written record** indicating subjects taught and activities engaged in; (b) a **portfolio of samples** of the child's academic work; (c) a **record of evaluations** of the child's academic progress — or other written/credible evidence equivalent to these. **Enforcement is prosecutorial only:** records are "subject to review **only by the local prosecuting attorney**" — not the district, not DESE. A **daily log** showing a compliant course of instruction is a **defense to any prosecution** under 167.031 or educational-neglect charges. Note: guides insisting on daily hour-logging as a legal mandate overstate the text — the daily log functions as an affirmative defense, not a standalone mandate.
   - **No testing is required.** **No parent qualifications.**
   - **FPE schools (RSMo 167.013):** a home school "does not enroll children who participate in the MOScholars/ESA program" — families using ESA funds are FPE schools, a separate category. If the parent mentions ESA funds, say so and tell them to verify the FPE-school rules (167.013) rather than relying on this module.
   - Graduation and diplomas: no state diploma; the parent determines completion and issues the family's diploma. **A+ Scholarship: homeschool graduates are NOT eligible** — it requires attending and graduating from a designated A+ high school.
   - Re-enrolling in public school: no statute mandates credit acceptance; the decision falls to local school boards (per DESE, under §171.011 — flagged as not primary-verified in the research, so hedge). Related: **RSMo 167.790** (2025) gives home-school and FPE-school students access to district events/activities through the statewide activities association — new law, no track record yet.
4. Close with the honest-limits line (this is public legal information, not legal advice; edge cases go to the district or an attorney) and, where relevant, the myth corrections: "file a declaration of enrollment" (repealed); "the law is at 167.031(2)" (moved to 167.012); 167.071 citations (repealed 2024); "Missouri requires daily hour logs" (overstated — the log is a defense, not a mandate); "A+ Scholarship is available to homeschoolers" (false); "umbrella school" requirements (no such concept in Missouri law); any "declaration of intent within 30 days" claim (no filing of any kind exists).
5. Watch-outs to re-check before the parent acts: whether the Children's Division can compel records in a child-welfare investigation (untested); what counts as "equivalent evidence" to the three record categories (never defined by a court — relying on the alternative carries legal risk); how the 1,000-hour rule is practically measured; the scope of 167.790's activities access in practice.

## Output

Direct answers to the parent's question. If the AI tool cannot save files, say so plainly and provide any checklist as copy-paste text.

## Boundaries

- Never legal advice. If the question goes beyond the verified facts — disputes with a district, special-education questions, truancy proceedings, or anything adversarial — say so honestly and point the parent to their school district or an attorney.
- Never presents the credit-transfer attribution, the Children's Division question, "equivalent evidence," or 167.790's practical scope as settled — all flagged as unverified/untested in the 2026-09-27 research.
- Never applies this module to ESA/MOScholars families — they're FPE schools under 167.013, a different category.
- Defers record-keeping and progress questions to the progress-tracking module; defers curriculum questions to the curriculum-and-resource-guidance module.
