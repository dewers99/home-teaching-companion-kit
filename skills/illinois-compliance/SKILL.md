# Illinois compliance skill

**Version:** 1.0.1
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27

## When to use

Use this module when the parent asks about Illinois home-education requirements: what the law requires, what to file, when the deadlines are, whether they're compliant, or how re-enrollment in public school works. If the parent's state isn't settled yet, or they want the cross-state picture, load `skills/compliance-core/` first. This module shares public legal facts and pointers to official sources only. It never gives legal advice, and for anything edge-case or disputed it tells the parent to check with their school district or an attorney.

## Procedure

1. Determine the situation: starting home education for the first time, leaving a public school, or a re-enrollment/college question. The answer differs by situation, so nail this down first.
2. Consult `reference/illinois-compliance-quick-reference.md` for the exact facts before answering — the module states WHEN to consult it and never volunteers the whole appendix unprompted. Give the parent only the piece they need for their situation.
3. Give the specific answer: what to file, when, where, and what to keep. The core facts to convey (facts researched 2026-09-27; do not embellish):
   - Governing law: Illinois has **no "homeschool statute."** Homeschools operate as **private schools** under the compulsory-attendance exemption in **105 ILCS 5/26-1**. The Illinois Supreme Court held in ***People v. Levisen* (1950)** that home schooling counts as private schooling "if the teacher were competent, the required subjects were taught, and the student received an education at least equivalent to public schooling" — ISBE's own page cites this decision. *Scoma v. Chicago Board of Education* (1974) reaffirmed it and placed the **burden of proof on the parents** to establish their program meets state requirements. Illinois has the lightest regulatory touch of all nine kit states.
   - Compulsory attendance: **ages 6 (on or before September 1) to 17** (since the 2014–2015 school year; older guides citing 7–16 or 7–17 are outdated).
   - **No registration is required.** ISBE verbatim: "The state of Illinois does not require parents to register before they begin home schooling." ISBE offers a **voluntary** registration form parents "are encouraged to complete" — "There are no other forms, documents, or procedures required by the state of Illinois." Treat any guide describing the voluntary form as mandatory (or "must be filed annually in September") as misleading.
   - **Leaving public school:** no formal withdrawal procedure is mandated, but ISBE **highly recommends** notifying the public school with a dated letter (keep copies), enclosing the voluntary registration form, and sending the same to the Regional Office of Education. Skipping this risks the school marking the child absent and referring the case to a truancy officer.
   - Required subjects: instruction must be provided in **Language Arts; Mathematics; Biological and Physical Science; Social Science (Social Studies); Fine Arts; and Physical Development and Health** — **in the English language** — and the education must be **at least equivalent** to what public schools offer. (The statutory branches list is commonly cited as 105 ILCS 5/27-1, but that citation could not be primary-verified on 2026-09-27 — the subject list itself is confirmed via ISBE.)
   - **No testing is required** — and any tests the parent chooses to give need not be submitted to anyone.
   - **No records are mandated by statute.** But the burden of proof matters: the regional superintendent investigates noncompliance reports and "may expect the parents… to establish, when necessary, that they are providing instruction that is at least commensurate with the standards established for public schools." A program that doesn't meet the standard can draw a truancy referral and a **Class C misdemeanor**. So the "records anyway" principle carries extra weight in Illinois: an attendance log and a portfolio of work samples are the parent's proof.
   - **No parent qualifications.**
   - Graduation and diplomas: **parent-determined** — no state diploma mechanism for homeschoolers (the exact phrasing rests on the statutory framework rather than a primary ISBE statement, so present it as the working rule, not a quoted mandate). College: the University of Illinois "encourage[s] you to apply" — roughly 60–70 homeschooled students admitted each year, reviewed individually, and **no GED required for domestic applicants** (international homeschooled applicants must submit one).
   - Re-enrolling in public school: the school determines grade placement — it may test the student or ask for proof of material covered. Districts **may not** require the program to be "registered" or "recognized" by the State Board, and may not make unreasonable or arbitrary placement decisions.
4. Close with the honest-limits line (this is public legal information, not legal advice; edge cases go to the district or an attorney) and, where relevant, the myth corrections: "you must register" (false — voluntary); "testing is required / scores must be submitted" (false); "the district must approve your curriculum" (false — ISBE: parents are "free to decide the manner, time and materials"); "compulsory age is 7–16" (outdated — 6 to 17 since 2014–15); and "**HB 2827 is law**" (false — see watch-outs).
5. Watch-outs to re-check before the parent acts: **HB 2827, the "Homeschool Act" (104th GA)** — not law as of 2026-09-27, but not fully dead: it passed a House committee in March 2025, was parked in the Rules Committee in April 2025, and the General Assembly runs through 2026, so it could theoretically revive. It would have required an annual Homeschool Declaration to the regional office, a high-school-diploma minimum for the homeschool administrator, and portfolio production on a truant officer's request. Present it as "watch this space with a re-check date," never as current law. Also re-verify 26-1's current text (a 2025 amendment's content was not checked).

## Output

Direct answers to the parent's question. If the AI tool cannot save files, say so plainly and provide any checklist as copy-paste text.

## Boundaries

- Never legal advice. If the question goes beyond the verified facts — disputes with a district, special-education questions, truancy proceedings, or anything adversarial — say so honestly and point the parent to their school district or an attorney.
- Never presents HB 2827 as law or as dead — it is parked and could revive; the module flags it for re-checking.
- Never presents the 27-1 citation or the diploma phrasing as primary-verified.
- Defers record-keeping and progress questions to the progress-tracking module; defers curriculum questions to the curriculum-and-resource-guidance module.
