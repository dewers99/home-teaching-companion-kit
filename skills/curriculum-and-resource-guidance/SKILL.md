# Curriculum and resource guidance skill

**Version:** 1.0.0

## When to use

Use this module when the parent asks what curriculum to use, what resources fit their family's approach, or how to choose materials. The kit bundles NO curriculum and endorses none — this module helps the parent recognize their own fit and points at resource types that match, leaning hard on free and low-cost options.

## Procedure

1. Check what intake recorded about the family's teaching approach and budget. If either is unknown, ask — never assume. A family doing hands-on, project-led learning and a family wanting a structured daily schedule get very different guidance.
2. Briefly describe how the major approaches differ in practice — one plain sentence each, describe don't prescribe — so the parent can recognize which one sounds like their family. The point is recognition, not a recommendation.
3. Suggest TYPES of resources that fit the stated approach and budget, leading with free or low-cost: the public library (interlibrary loan is the great underrated tool), household materials, public-domain texts, community programs like museums, parks, and library programs. Paid options come after free ones, in that order. For Pre-K supply questions, build the list from `reference/prek-supplies.md` — starter kit first, then categories, always with the household alternative alongside the buy option. For K–2, use `reference/k-2-supplies.md` — start from what the family already owns, since most K–2 needs are upgrades, not a fresh start. For 3–5, use `reference/3-5-supplies.md` — organization and subject-specific tools are the theme; flashcards and index cards are the highest-leverage cheap buys. For 6–8, use `reference/6-8-supplies.md` — the theme is systems for independence; the planner, index cards, and graph paper matter more than anything expensive. For 9–12, use `reference/9-12-supplies.md` — specialize to the student's courses and direction, never hand over the whole list; the graphing calculator and a working laptop are the two big-ticket items.
4. Well-known curricula or programs may be named as EXAMPLES across approaches, but never present any single one as the right choice and never rank commercial products. Every mention ties back to the family's approach, the child's age or level, and the budget — "this fits families like yours because..." not "this is the best."
5. For Pre-K resource questions, work from the domain structure in `reference/prek-domains.md` — suggest resource types per domain, and treat worksheet collections as the short practice-page layer, never the whole program.
6. Offer to help compare two or three specific options the parent is already considering, laying out honest trade-offs (cost, prep time, structure level, fit for the child's level). If the parent hasn't narrowed it down, stay at the resource-type level until they do.

## Output

A shortlist of resource types and/or examples with why each fits the family's approach, budget, and the child's age or level, in chat. Keep it short enough to act on — five options with one line of fit each beats a catalogue.

## Boundaries

- Does not sell or endorse products, and does not claim any curriculum is "best" or the right choice for the family.
- Does not write or bundle a full curriculum — this module points at materials; it doesn't create them.
- Defers lesson planning to the lesson-and-unit-planning module and legal questions to the compliance module.
- If the parent asks for resources the kit cannot evaluate (e.g., a brand-new program with no track record), say so honestly rather than inventing an opinion.
